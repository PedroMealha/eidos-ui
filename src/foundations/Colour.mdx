## Text roles

<GuideNote title="Use a text role, never a raw --gray-N" hint="SC 1.4.3">
  The greys are a *surface* ramp. Reaching into them for a label is how the library once ended up
  with 99 declarations of `color: var(--gray-400)` on placeholders, hints and timestamps - `#94a3b8`
  measures 2.56:1 on white, against the 4.5:1 that SC 1.4.3 requires. `--text-disabled` is the one
  deliberate exception: disabled controls are exempt, and are *supposed* to look unavailable.
</GuideNote>

## Palette

Each base is chosen so that white text on it clears 4.5:1 with a little margin - they all sit near
5:1. That headroom is deliberate and thin, which is why muted or faded text on a filled surface does
not work: there is nothing left to spend. Pick a foreground with `--x-contrast` rather than
hardcoding white.

## Dark scheme

Every value above is the **light** scheme's. `<html data-color-scheme="dark">` - or `"system"`, when the operating system prefers dark - re-points the surfaces, the text roles, the grey ramp and the whole palette. Switch the toolbar's **Scheme** control to see any story in it.

The dark palette is not hand-picked. Each base is its light colour lightened, hue kept, until it clears 5:1 against both dark surfaces, and its foreground flips to dark text. It is generated by the same functions `ThemeProvider` uses to darken a custom colour, so a themed app's dark mode meets the same bar as the preset. Unit tests hold the stylesheet to those functions and check every text, fill, hover and focus-ring pairing in both schemes; the accessibility audit renders every story in both.

Components paint with `--surface`, `--surface-raised` (menus, dialogs, popovers) and `--text-default`. The literals - `--white`, `--dark-color` - keep their values in both schemes, so use the roles in your own CSS if it should follow the scheme.
